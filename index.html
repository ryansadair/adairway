What the numbers say

Log a few rounds and this section will start surfacing patterns.

Handicap index best differentials, last 20 rounds

Score trend

Each ● tan dot is one round's score relative to par (0 = even par, above the line = over par, below = under par). A ○ hollow dot is a 9-hole round, doubled so it plots on the same scale as an 18. When you filter a year, the axis is that full year (Jan–Dec) with monthly ticks; All time pads to the months you actually logged. ┊ green dashed lines mark practice sessions. Tap or hover a dot or practice line for the exact date and value.

Putts per round

Same idea as the score chart — tan dots are individual rounds. Hollow dots are 9-hole rounds, doubled to compare against an 18. Green dashed lines mark practice. Same time axis as Score trend. Tap or hover a dot or practice line for the exact date and value.

Fairways hit %

One round per dot, oldest to newest, left to right. Higher is better — more tee shots landing in the fairway.

Greens in regulation %

One round per dot, oldest to newest. Higher is better — more approach shots reaching the green in regulation.

Scoring by par type

Your average score relative to par on 3s, 4s, and 5s, plus how often you make birdie-or-better, par, bogey, or worse — this is where to look to see which hole type is actually costing you strokes.

Putts by distance

Logged from the "1st putt" tag in Play/Rounds — how far your first putt was on each green, whether you got there in regulation or scrambled after a miss. Tap a bucket, or type an exact number of feet and the right bucket gets picked for you automatically. This is the difference between a GIR-and-2-putt from 25 feet (normal — very few golfers make those) and a missed green where a sharp chip left you a tap-in. The distance is what should drive whether a 2-putt feels like a miss or not.

FIR% vs score

Each dot is one round: further right = more fairways hit that round, lower = a better score (vs. par) that round. If your best-scoring rounds cluster toward the bottom-right, driving accuracy is genuinely paying off for you — if not, it may not be your biggest lever.

GIR% vs score

Same read as the chart on the left, but for greens in regulation instead of fairways — further right and lower together suggests GIR is the bigger driver of your good rounds.
